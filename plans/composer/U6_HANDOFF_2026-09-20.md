## 3. Three findings worth your attention

**A guard I wrote and then deleted.** `select()` began with a check for "another
layer already holds a selection → replace instead of union". Mutation S4
survived, and the reason is not a missing test: `selectOnly` guarantees at most
one layer holds a selection, so when another layer holds one *this* layer's is
empty — and a union with empty is exactly the replacement the guard performed.
It could not change an outcome. Deleted, with the reasoning left in the comment;
the behaviour is still gated by `AddingAcrossLayersReplacesRatherThanSplitting`,
which passes because `selectOnly` enforces it.

**A real null dereference the falsifier found.** Removing the `!target` check
(Add/Toggle on a layer with no features — a basemap) **segfaults**. There was no
gate for it until the mutation went looking. `ALayerWithNoFeaturesIsNotSelectedInto`
now covers it, and mutation S5 is caught by exit status, not by output — the
C4c lesson, earning its keep a third time.

**`DifferenceLayer` deliberately does not implement `IComponentLayer`.** It is
derived from two runs and belongs to no single component; answering "which
component" would be half true. `componentOf` returns empty for it, and the hub
then asks the canvas to select nothing, which is honest.

## 5. Look at it

Load a composition with components that publish spatial outputs, then
*Map ▸ Data ▸ From Components*.

1. **Canvas → layers.** Click a component box on the Composition tab. The
   Layers dock raises and that component's layer becomes the current row. Click
   a component with no layers: the current row does not change (it is not
   cleared — that would throw away a selection you made).
2. **Layers → canvas.** Switch to the Map, click a feature. The canvas selects
   the component that produced it, and the Arguments dock follows (it already
   followed the canvas). Click a feature on a layer you loaded from a file: the
   canvas selection clears rather than staying lit on the last component.
3. **No ping-pong.** Alternate between the two a dozen times. Nothing recurses,
   nothing flickers, and the selection does not drift.
4. **Additive selection, map.** Click a feature; Shift-click another — both are
   highlighted and the attribute table shows two rows. ⌘-click one of them —
   it drops out. ⌘-click it again — back in. Plain click — back to one.
5. **Additive selection, band.** Drag a rubber band with Shift held: what it
   catches joins the selection rather than replacing it.
6. **Across layers.** With features selected on one layer, Shift-click a
   feature on another: the first layer's selection clears and the new one
   stands alone. (One layer at a time is what lets the table show it.)
7. **Basemap.** Shift-click on a basemap or a raster: the selection clears and
   nothing crashes — this is the path mutation S5 turns into a segfault.
8. **Run browser.** Open a run, *Show on Map* an item. The layer is added, the
   tree makes it current, and if that component is on the canvas it is selected.
