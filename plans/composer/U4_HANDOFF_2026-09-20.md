## 2. Two gaps from D31 are narrower than they were

**The cue is pixel-testable offscreen.** `renderSceneToImage()` drives the same
`SceneRenderer::render()` the widget does, and the cue is drawn inside it — so
`tests/gui/test_gizmodraw.cpp` checks, in pixels and under the offscreen
platform, that the cue appears at all, that it appears in the corner it was
asked for (all four), that switching it off leaves that corner untouched, and
that turning the camera changes the picture. That is most of what would
otherwise have been "confirm by eye".

**`scenerenderer.cpp` and `sceneview.cpp` now syntax-check off-machine.** Both
were unreachable because `<rhi/qrhi.h>` is Qt 6.7+ and the container has 6.4.
Qt 6.4 ships the same class privately, so the harness gained a three-line shim
(`shims/rhi/qrhi.h` → `QtGui/6.4.2/QtGui/private/qrhi_p.h`) and the two private
include roots. Both files are **CLEAN**. `plans/setup_syntax_check.sh` records
it. This helps every future RHI slice, not only this one.

What still cannot be checked here: whether the depth squeeze actually wins
against real scene depths on Metal, and whether the cue *looks* right.

## 3. Five defects my own gates caught

**The gizmo re-derived the camera's eye, and two gates paid for it.** At exactly
90° of elevation the view direction is parallel to the up vector and
`QMatrix4x4::lookAt` collapses to a matrix that maps every arm onto the origin —
so a plan view showed no cue at all. `Camera::setElevation` already clamps to
89.999 for precisely this reason. The cue now builds a real `Camera` at unit
distance and takes `eye()` from it, so the convention *and* the guard live in
one place. The comment in that function about a sign convention written twice is
now true rather than aspirational.

**`copysign` is not a comparison when the component is zero.** The
camera-agreement gate compared signs and failed at azimuth 180 and 270, where
one side gave `+0.0` and the other `-0.0`. Probing showed the two agree
*exactly* — every component is the camera's divided by 1.4, the cue's
orthographic half-width — so the gate asserts that ratio. Stronger than what it
replaced, and it has no zero to trip over.

**`QRect::isEmpty()` — the degenerate-rectangle trap, fifth occurrence.**
Deleting the `side <= 0` guard hands the renderer a rect of width **−16**, and
`isEmpty()` calls that empty exactly as it calls width 0 empty. The mutation
survived a gate written the obvious way. Gates on a "nothing to draw" rectangle
now assert a width of *zero*. (Previously: `map/extentmath.h` in D26, point
bounds in C4a, `MeshSpatialGrid` in C2, the marker extent in U3.)

**An inverted rectangle is more dangerous than an empty one.** I had guessed the
opposite. An empty rect divides by zero and yields ±infinity, which every later
comparison rejects; an inverted one divides cleanly and yields a plausible point
*inside* the square — so a press nowhere near the cue answers with an arm and
swings the camera. The gate uses that input now.

**The tolerance was stated twice** — as an early `continue` and again as
`bestDistance`'s seed — so either could be deleted with nothing noticing.
Removed the duplicate rather than writing a test for it.

## 4. Two design notes worth your eye

**The corner arithmetic and the Y-flip are outside the widget.**
`axisGizmoRect()` and `axisGizmoPoint()` are free functions, so the whole click
path — view size and corner to a rect, widget press to cue point, point to arm —
is checkable without a device, and it is where a north/south flip would have
hidden. `SceneView::takeGizmoPress` is three calls and no arithmetic of its own.
The renderer is handed *the same rectangle* scaled by the device pixel ratio
rather than recomputing it, because a corner worked out twice is a corner that
will eventually be two corners — and on a retina display the 12px inset alone
would differ.

**The cue is depth-squeezed, not depth-disabled.** `QRhi` offers no mid-pass
depth clear, and a second pass would clear the colour too. Turning depth off
would make the arms draw in submission order, putting Up in front of East
whichever way the camera faced. So the viewport's depth range is compressed into
the nearest hundredth of the buffer: every fragment of the cue beats every
fragment of the scene, while the arms keep their depths *relative to each other*
and occlude one another correctly. **This is the thing I most want you to look
at**, because it either works or leaves the cue invisible behind terrain, and no
arithmetic here can tell you which. `test_gizmodraw` draws an opaque sheet
filling the whole view and then looks for red, green and blue in the corner.

## 6. Look at it

```bash
build/darwin/composer_screenshot artifacts/composer-gizmo.png --scene
```

Then in the app, with a mesh loaded and the **3D** tab open:

1. The cue is in the **bottom-left** corner: a red arrow to the east, green to
   the north, blue up. Orbit with the left button — the cue turns with the
   camera and stays the same size. This is the whole point of it.
2. **Zoom right in and right out.** The cue must not change size and must not
   move off its corner. If it grows with the scene, the camera's distance has
   leaked into its matrix.
3. **Set vertical exaggeration to 10** (3D View preferences, or the ribbon). The
   terrain stretches; the cue must not. A north arrow that stretched would be
   telling the user something false about the ground.
4. **Tilt to straight down.** Three arms, not none — the blue one collapsed to a
   dot in the middle, the other two at right angles. If the cue vanishes here,
   the camera clamp is not being borrowed.
5. **Click the green arm.** The camera swings to look north, level. Click it
   again: nothing moves, which is the stability property. Click red: looking
   west from the east. Click blue: straight down, *without* the map spinning —
   a plan view has no meaningful azimuth and changing it would answer a question
   nobody asked.
6. **Click inside the square but not on an arm.** Nothing happens, and crucially
   the scene does **not** start orbiting — the press was aimed at a control.
7. **Drive the cue over a hill.** Orbit until terrain fills the corner the cue
   is in. It must stay fully visible. This is the depth squeeze; if part of an
   arm disappears into the ground, tell me and I will move it to its own pass.
8. **Preferences ▸ 3D View**: untick *Show the orientation cue* — it goes, and
   the size and corner rows grey out. Tick it back. Move it to **Top right** —
   it moves immediately, no restart. Set the size to 24 and to 256.
9. **Shrink the 3D panel** until it is smaller than the cue. The cue shrinks to
   fit; it must never hang over the edge or vanish in a flicker of artefacts.
